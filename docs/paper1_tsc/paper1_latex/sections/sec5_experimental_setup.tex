\subsection{Simulation Environment and Mixed-Traffic Calibration}
\label{sec:sim_env}

All experiments are conducted in SUMO~\cite{lopez2018_sumo}, configured with
the sub-lane model at a lateral resolution of $0.4$~m. This choice is deliberate
rather than cosmetic: in motorcycle-dominant traffic the conventional one-vehicle-per-lane
abstraction is invalid, as powered two-wheelers continuously filter laterally and form
wide, deformable clusters that conventional car-following models cannot reproduce. The
sub-lane resolution restores the lateral degrees of freedom required to capture this
filtering behaviour, consistent with microscopic observations of motorcycle dynamics under
Vietnamese mixed-traffic conditions~\cite{vn_roundabout}.

The vehicle composition is a central calibration target. The motorcycle count share is set
to $73\%$---anchored to the $72.6\%$ Hanoi mode share of
Ngoc~\emph{et~al.}~\cite{ngoc2021_fivecities}, who report $\approx 80$--$89\%$ for four
further Vietnamese cities, a range deliberately bracketed by the $\{50\%, 73\%, 90\%\}$
composition sweep of Section~\ref{sec:generalization}---with the remainder allocated to
passenger cars ($17\%$), trucks ($7\%$), and buses ($3\%$). Count share is distinguished
from traffic \emph{load}: at a motorcycle passenger-car-unit (PCU) equivalence of
$\approx 0.29$~\cite{cao_sano_hanoi}, the $73\%$ count share carries only about one-third
of the PCU-based load, so count share informs the lateral-filtering arguments and PCU the
capacity arguments. With \texttt{latAlignment=arbitrary} and a calibrated minimum lateral
gap \texttt{minGapLat}, three to four motorcycles share a standard $3.2$~m urban lane
alongside a passenger car, and a queue-discharge experiment yields $\approx 1500$~PCU/h
per lane---characteristic of the Hanoi mixed-flow regime~\cite{cao_sano_hanoi}. The
$73\%$ value is thus a representative anchor, not a universal constant, and robustness to
compositional shift is defended by the sweep of Section~\ref{sec:generalization}.

\subsubsection{Signal-Timing Bounds}
The minimum green time is fixed at $15$~s (three decision intervals), ensuring that an
activated phase discharges a meaningful platoon and suppressing the high-frequency
flickering that otherwise traps wide motorcycle clusters within the intersection. The
maximum green time is fixed at $60$~s via a $100$k-step design-selection pilot on the N3
grid, in which the $60$~s bound dominates a relaxed $90$~s bound by $15$--$25\%$ across
all primary metrics (the pilot table is provided in the supplementary material), because
excessively long greens starve cross-phase movements, conflicting with the
anti-starvation objective of Section~\ref{sec:reward}. Both bounds are structural choices
applied uniformly to every learned and heuristic controller; the maximum green also sets
the scale of the \texttt{green\_timer\_norm} feature, and the SUMO-actuated controller's
\texttt{maxDur} is matched to $60$~s for fairness.

\subsection{Networks and Demand}
\label{sec:networks}
The framework is evaluated on two synthetic but structurally sound topologies: a
signalized corridor (denoted N2; $1 \times 5$ junctions) and a signalized grid
(denoted N3; $4 \times 4$ junctions). All intersections are four-way with two lanes per approach. The two scales
jointly probe both arterial coordination (the corridor) and network-level interaction under
many simultaneously learning agents (the grid). On the corridor, each agent's observation is
additionally augmented with the upstream neighbor's phase one-hot and training uses a shared
team-mean reward---two standard coordination aids for arterial progression; the grid arms use
the base formulation unchanged.

To stress-test the controllers under non-stationary load rather than a single operating
point, vehicle insertions over the $5400$~s episode are drawn from an inhomogeneous Poisson
process following a time-varying trapezoidal profile. The profile ramps from an off-peak lull
to a sustained demand peak, eases to an intermediate plateau, and tapers back, exercising the
controllers across loading, saturation, and unloading regimes. The exact mathematical
formulation and rate parameters are provided in the supplementary material.

\subsection{Baselines}
\label{sec:baselines}
The proposed MAPPO controller is benchmarked against a spectrum of strong, non-strawman
baselines, grouped into two sensing classes. \emph{Camera-deployable} methods---fixed-time,
Webster, max-pressure, SOTL, IPPO, and the proposed MAPPO---act on quantities estimable from
roadside detection and share the same phase groups, $5$~s decision interval, and
minimum/maximum-green bounds. \emph{Instrumented references}---SUMO-actuated and
MAPPO-privileged---are additionally granted sensing that a camera-only deployment does not
possess (per-second in-road gap detection and exact simulator state, respectively) and serve
as upper anchors rather than deployable competitors.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Fixed-time}: an equal-split cycle ($30$~s per phase group), representing the
        legacy plan prevalent at the target intersections.
  \item \emph{Webster fixed-time}~\cite{webster1958}: an analytical plan from PCU-weighted
        measured flows, $C = (1.5L + 5)/(1 - Y)$ clamped to $[40, 120]$~s with proportional
        splits; its saturation flow is calibrated to the measured sublane capacity
        ($\approx 1500$~PCU/h/lane), so the comparison is not degraded by car-only
        assumptions.
  \item \emph{Max-Pressure}~\cite{varaiya2013_maxpressure}: a greedy pressure heuristic on
        the identical observation pipeline and phase groups, serving the phase of highest
        pressure.
  \item \emph{SOTL}~\cite{cools2013_sotl}: holds green until the red-approach count exceeds
        $\kappa = 5$ after the minimum green, with a forced switch at the maximum green;
        counts are read from the same proxy pipeline.
  \item \emph{SUMO-actuated} (instrumented reference): SUMO's native gap-based
        actuation~\cite{lopez2018_sumo} (\texttt{minDur}~$=15$~s, \texttt{maxDur}~$=60$~s,
        \texttt{max-gap}~$=3$~s) evaluated at $1$~s resolution---per-second in-road sensing
        that the $5$~s camera pipeline cannot reproduce, hence an instrumented reference.
  \item \emph{IPPO}~\cite{dewitt2020_ippo}: identical actor architecture and budget but
        independent per-agent critics on the local observation, isolating the CTDE
        contribution.
  \item \emph{MAPPO-privileged (upper bound)}: the identical MAPPO~\cite{yu2022_mappo}
        algorithm with the observation augmented to $38$ dimensions of exact simulator
        quantities and the reward held fixed; its gap to the proxy policy quantifies the
        \emph{information-restriction cost} of Section~\ref{sec:restriction_cost}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Metrics and Statistical Protocol}
\label{sec:metrics}
Performance is quantified by two primary metrics extracted from SUMO's \texttt{tripinfo}
output: mean travel time and mean waiting time. Secondary metrics comprise the P95 waiting
time (which captures starvation events masked by the mean) and the number of completed
trips (throughput). Environmental indicators
(CO\textsubscript{2} emissions and fuel consumption, computed via the HBEFA-based emission
model~\cite{krajzewicz2015_emissions}) are evaluated post hoc and are never exposed to the
agent reward.

Each learned method is trained with five independent seeds ($42, 123, 456, 789, 1337$)
and each checkpoint is evaluated on five held-out demand route-seeds per scenario---one
sample is a checkpoint's route-seed mean, so the reported variability reflects
policy-training variance---while the deterministic heuristics are evaluated once per
route-seed. All tables report the mean with a $95\%$ confidence interval ($n = 5$);
significance against each baseline is assessed with a two-sided Mann--Whitney $U$
test~\cite{mann1947_mwu} under Holm--Bonferroni correction~\cite{holm1979}, with effect
size reported through Cohen's~$d$~\cite{cohen1988}. Because $n = 5$ seeds gives the
corrected test limited power, effect sizes, not $p$-values alone, are treated as the
primary evidence of consistency.

\subsection{Training Configuration and Reproducibility}
\label{sec:training}
Each model is trained to a fixed budget of $3 \times 10^{5}$ environment steps, a
horizon established by convergence pilots. Observation statistics are normalized by
Welford's algorithm~\cite{welford1962} and serialized into the checkpoint, so that
evaluation and deployment apply identical normalization without recompilation. The complete
set of MAPPO training hyperparameters---including PPO clip ratios, GAE discount factors, and
learning rates---is detailed in the supplementary material.

To guarantee end-to-end reproducibility, the full codebase, trained checkpoints,
demand generators (\texttt{.rou.xml}), phase-group derivations, and evaluation scripts are
released publicly at \mbox{[REPOSITORY~URL]} under a tagged commit. The precise observation
and reward schema version is stamped into every run configuration, ensuring that all
results are traceable to an exact environment specification.
