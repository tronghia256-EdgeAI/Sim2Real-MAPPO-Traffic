\section{Proposed Methodology}

\subsection{Queue-Centric Vision-Proxy Reward}
\label{sec:reward}
A central contribution of this work is the formulation of a reward function that is
strictly computable from camera-derived proxies, ensuring that the objective optimized
during simulation coincides with the metric available at deployment. The governing design
rule is that every reward term $r^{(k)}_i(t)$ factors through the vision-proxy projection
$\phi(\mathbf{s}_t)$ introduced in Section~III-B. Unlike prior vision-based reinforcement
learning, which pairs a camera-constrained state with a privileged simulator reward
(typically the exact accumulated delay), no term in the proposed reward reads a privileged
quantity---the accumulated-delay penalty included, which is realized through a
halted-queue proxy. The objective is therefore deployable by construction.

The proposed reward $R_i(t)$ for intersection $i$ is a \emph{queue-centric} objective---a
congestion-minimization core, regularized by a throughput term and a switching term---written
as a weighted sum of six terms:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
R_i(t)
=&\;
w_q \frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{l=1}^{n_i} q_l^2
+w_p\, p_i(t)
+w_T\, T_i(t) \\
&+
w_s\, \mathbb{1}_{[\text{switch}]}
+w_v \max(\tau-\bar{v},0)
+w_w\, \bar{w}(t),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:reward}
\end{equation}
where the weights are empirically set to
$w_q=-1.0$,
$w_p=-0.5$,
$w_T=+1.0$,
$w_s=-0.1$,
$w_v=-0.2$,
and
$w_w=-0.3$.
To ensure stable temporal-difference learning, the final scalar is clipped to
$[-2.0,\, +1.5]$. The individual components are detailed below.

We emphasize that these six terms are \emph{not} six orthogonal objectives. Four of them are
monotone functions of a single congestion signal---the per-approach halted-queue fraction $q_l$
(\texttt{effective\_queue\_norm}). The queue penalty applies its $L_2$ norm
($\frac{1}{n_i}\sum_l q_l^2$, the Jensen anti-starvation form derived below), whereas the
accumulated-delay penalty $\bar{w}(t)$ applies the $L_1$ norm of the \emph{same} vector
($\frac{1}{n_i}\sum_l q_l$): the per-vehicle waiting timer is never read, so in code this term
reduces exactly to the mean halted-queue fraction. The low-speed penalty and the
phase-imbalance pressure term likewise rise monotonically with this congestion state. The
reward is therefore best understood as dense, multi-scale gradient shaping around a
queue-minimization core, in which the local ROI-exit throughput reward and the phase-switch
penalty are the two genuinely distinct regularizers. The marginal value of each shaping term
\emph{beyond} the $L_2$ queue core---rather than an assumption of independence---is what the
ablations of Section~VI-F quantify.

\paragraph{Anti-starvation queue penalty}
Rather than minimizing the linear mean queue length across the $n_i$ controlled lanes,
the mean of squared queue lengths $\frac{1}{n_i}\sum_{l=1}^{n_i} q_l^2$ is penalized.
By Jensen's inequality,
\begin{equation}
\frac{1}{n_i} \sum_{l=1}^{n_i} q_l^2 \;\geq\; \left(\frac{1}{n_i} \sum_{l=1}^{n_i} q_l\right)^2,
\label{eq:jensen}
\end{equation}
with equality holding strictly when the per-lane queues are perfectly balanced; the gap
between the two quantities is exactly the queue variance. Consequently, the quadratic
formulation implicitly penalizes spatial imbalance and discourages the starvation of
minor approaches. For illustration, a highly imbalanced distribution $q = (1,0,0,0)$ incurs
a penalty of $0.25$, whereas the balanced distribution $q = (0.25,0.25,0.25,0.25)$ incurs
only $0.0625$---a four-fold reduction---despite identical total demand.

\paragraph{Phase-imbalance pressure}
A signed \emph{phase-imbalance} term $p_i(t) \in [-1, 1]$ measures the queue delta between the
two fixed phase groups at the \emph{same} junction, partitioned by the currently red and green
movements:
\begin{equation}
p_i(t) = \frac{1}{n_i} \left( \sum_{l \in \text{red}} q_l - \sum_{l \in \text{green}} q_l \right).
\label{eq:pressure}
\end{equation}
This is an \emph{intra-junction} demand-imbalance gradient and is explicitly \emph{not} the
upstream--downstream network pressure of classical max-pressure
control~\cite{varaiya2013_maxpressure}; accordingly we do not claim the throughput-optimality
guarantee of max-pressure routing, and---compounded by per-lane normalization and value
clipping---the term functions as a highly reactive local heuristic rather than an optimal
routing policy. In contrast to a one-sided $\max(p, 0)$ surrogate, which provides zero gradient
whenever the active allocation is already correct, the signed form supplies a continuous
gradient that rewards serving the more congested group and penalizes serving the emptier one.

A naming subtlety must be made explicit, because the reward pressure and the observation
feature of the same name are reconciled rather than identical. The reward pressure $p_i(t)$ is
\emph{signal-relative}: it is evaluated over the dynamic red/green partition induced by the
active phase, as the normalized sum $\bigl(\sum_{\text{red}} q_l - \sum_{\text{green}} q_l\bigr)/n_i$.
The observation feature $\mathrm{pressure\_norm}$ of Section~\ref{sec:obs_space} is instead
\emph{geometry-relative}: it encodes the static group-mean imbalance
$\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1 + \bar{q}_A - \bar{q}_B\bigr)$ over the fixed groups $A, B$. The two are
bridged exactly by the phase one-hot vector: since the one-hot identifies which fixed group
currently holds green, and $\mathrm{pressure\_norm}$ supplies $\bar{q}_A - \bar{q}_B = 2\,
\mathrm{pressure\_norm} - 1$, the signal-relative reward pressure is an affine, phase-determined
transform of these two observed quantities (exact up to the equal-group-size scaling of the
symmetric two-phase plans used here and the boundary saturation). The actor's observation
therefore contains all information that its pressure reward optimizes.

\paragraph{Local ROI-exit throughput}
The agent is rewarded for vehicles actively clearing the intersection. The throughput term
is defined as
\begin{equation}
T_i(t) = \mathrm{clip}\!\left(\frac{\bigl|\, \text{prev\_ids}(L_i) \setminus \text{cur\_ids}(L_i)\, \bigr|}{\kappa},\; 0,\; 1\right),
\label{eq:throughput}
\end{equation}
with normalizing capacity $\kappa = 20$ and $L_i$ the set of lanes controlled by agent $i$.
Crucially, the simulator computation---the set difference of per-lane vehicle identities
between consecutive steps in SUMO~\cite{lopez2018_sumo}---is \emph{semantically identical}
to the deployable measurement of track identities terminating at the boundary of a
ByteTrack~\cite{zhang2022_bytetrack} region of interest (ROI). The reward signal is thus a
deployable metric in itself, incurring no domain shift. Because an exit is registered for
any identity present at the previous step and absent at the current one, lateral lane
changes (frequent under the sublane model) and the rare SUMO teleport are also counted as
exits; this is a bounded source of measurement noise that the deployable ByteTrack ROI-exit
counter shares identically, and therefore introduces no train/deploy discrepancy.

\paragraph{Auxiliary terms}
A phase-switch penalty ($w_s = -0.1$) discourages high-frequency phase flickering, and a
low-speed penalty ($w_v = -0.2$) activates only when the mean approach speed $\bar{v}$
falls below a threshold $\tau$, targeting stop-and-go congestion. Finally, the
accumulated-delay penalty $\bar{w}(t)$ is realized as the mean halted-queue fraction---the
same $\phi$-computable signal as the queue feature, but averaged linearly rather than
squared. It supplies a dense low-queue gradient that complements the quadratic
anti-starvation term while remaining fully camera-computable, as no per-vehicle waiting
timer is read at any point. Its marginal contribution beyond the quadratic queue term is
isolated by the delay-term ablation reported in Section~VI-F.

\subsection{MAPPO Architecture and Optimization}
\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{assets/figures/system_architecture.pdf}
\caption{Overall architecture of the proposed camera-observable multi-agent control
framework. Solid paths denote the on-camera deployment data flow (no simulator): per-agent

\label{fig:architecture}

To optimize the multi-agent control policy, Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization
(MAPPO)~\cite{yu2022_mappo} is adopted---a standard instance of the Centralized Training
with Decentralized Execution (CTDE) paradigm for the Dec-POMDP~\cite{oliehoek2016_decpomdp}
formalized in Section~III. It is emphasized that the reinforcement-learning architecture
itself is standard and is not claimed as a methodological contribution; rather, it serves
as the vehicle through which the proposed camera-constrained state and vision-proxy reward
are evaluated.

The framework comprises a parameter-shared actor $\pi_\theta(a_{i,t}\,|\,\mathbf{o}_{i,t})$
that operates on the $26$-dimensional local proxy observation, yielding homogeneous control
behavior across intersections and facilitating zero-shot transfer to unseen topologies.
During training, a centralized critic $V_\psi(\mathbf{s}_t)$ ingests the concatenated global
proxy state. To preserve agent-specific reward shaping, the critic employs a multi-head
architecture that emits one value estimate per agent; this distinguishes the method from
the independent-critic IPPO~\cite{dewitt2020_ippo} baseline, in which each agent estimates
value from its local observation alone. Coordination in this design is achieved
\emph{solely} through the centralized critic: the per-agent rewards $r_{i,t}$ are strictly
local---each agent is credited only for the queues, phase imbalance, and ROI exits on its own
approaches, matching the deployment camera's field of view---so no inter-agent term appears in
the reward itself. Cross-intersection credit assignment enters only via the concatenated global
state ingested by the critic during centralized training and is absent at decentralized
execution, consistent both with the CTDE paradigm and with the camera-only constraint on the
deployed actor. Advantages $\hat{A}_{i,t}$ are computed by per-agent
Generalized Advantage Estimation (GAE)~\cite{schulman2016_gae} on the decentralized reward
streams $r_{i,t}$ and are subsequently standardized jointly across all agents within the
batch to stabilize the policy update:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{A}_{i,t}
&=
\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}
(\gamma\lambda)^l\,\delta_{i,t+l},
\\
\delta_{i,t}
&=
r_{i,t}
+\gamma V_\psi(\mathbf{s}_{t+1})_i
-
V_\psi(\mathbf{s}_t)_i .
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:gae}
\end{equation}
The policy is updated with the PPO clipped surrogate objective~\cite{schulman2017_ppo},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L^{\text{clip}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}
\Big[
\min \Big(
\rho_{i,t}\hat{A}_{i,t},
\;
&
\\
\mathrm{clip}
(
\rho_{i,t},
1-\epsilon,
1+\epsilon
)
\hat{A}_{i,t}
&
\Big)
\Big].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ppoclip}
\end{equation}
where $\rho_{i,t} = \pi_\theta(a_{i,t}|\mathbf{o}_{i,t}) / \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(a_{i,t}|\mathbf{o}_{i,t})$
is the importance ratio and $\epsilon = 0.2$. The critic is trained with a clipped,
per-head value loss,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
L^{\text{VF}}(\psi)
=
\frac{1}{N}
\sum_{i=1}^{N}
\mathbb{E}
\Big[
\max
\Big(
&
\\
\big(
V_\psi(\mathbf{s}_t)_i
-
\hat{R}_{i,t}
\big)^2,
&
\\
\big(
V^{\text{clip}}_{\psi}
(\mathbf{s}_t)_i
-
\hat{R}_{i,t}
\big)^2
&
\Big)
\Big].
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:valueloss}
\end{equation}
with early stopping triggered whenever the approximate KL divergence between successive
policies exceeds $0.015$. The complete training loop is summarized in Algorithm~\ref{alg:mappo}.

To bridge the residual normalization gap between training and deployment, the observation
running mean and variance are tracked online by Welford's algorithm~\cite{welford1962} and
serialized directly into the deployment checkpoint alongside the actor weights. Test-time
inputs $\mathbf{o}_{i,t}$ are therefore normalized with statistics identical to those seen
during training, without any dependence on live estimation.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{MAPPO with Per-Agent GAE for Traffic Signal Control}
\label{alg:mappo}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Initialize parameters $\theta, \psi$
\STATE Initialize Welford observation running mean/variance statistics
\FOR{iteration $= 1, 2, \ldots, \text{max\_iterations}$}
    \STATE Reset environments and obtain initial observations
    \FOR{step $t = 1$ to $128$}
        \STATE Normalize local observations $\mathbf{o}_{i,t}$ with running statistics
        \STATE Sample actions $a_{i,t} \sim \pi_\theta(\cdot\,|\,\mathbf{o}_{i,t})$ for each agent $i$
        \STATE Execute joint action $\mathbf{a}_t$; the environment applies the $3$\,s yellow and the min/max-green constraints
        \STATE Observe rewards $r_{i,t}$ and next observations $\mathbf{o}_{i,t+1}$
        \STATE Store transitions in per-agent buffers
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Update Welford running statistics with the collected batch
    \FOR{each agent $i$}
        \STATE Compute GAE advantages $\hat{A}_{i,t}$
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE Standardize advantages $\hat{A}_{i,t}$ jointly across all agents
    \FOR{epoch $= 1$ to $10$}
        \STATE Compute clipped surrogate loss $L^{\text{clip}}(\theta)$ across all agents
        \STATE Compute multi-head value loss $L^{\text{VF}}(\psi)$
        \STATE Update $\theta, \psi$ with the Adam optimizer
        \IF{approximate KL divergence $> 0.015$}
            \STATE Break (early stop)
        \ENDIF
    \ENDFOR
\ENDFOR
\STATE Serialize $\pi_\theta$ and the observation normalization statistics for deployment
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
