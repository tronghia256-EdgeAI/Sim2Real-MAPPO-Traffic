\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate every controller on the N2 corridor and the N3 grid under the time-varying
peak-demand profile of Section~\ref{sec:networks}, reporting the mean across five training
seeds with $95\%$ confidence intervals and assessing significance against the strongest
baseline with the Mann--Whitney $U$ test under Holm--Bonferroni correction
(Section~\ref{sec:metrics}). The analysis proceeds from the headline comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:res_quantitative}) to the cost of the camera-only restriction
(Section~\ref{sec:restriction_cost}), robustness under sensing noise
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness_noise}), generalization to compositional and demand shift
(Section~\ref{sec:generalization}), and component ablations (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).

\subsection{Main Performance Comparison}
\label{sec:res_quantitative}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports the primary and secondary metrics for all methods on both
networks, and Fig.~\ref{fig:main_comparison} visualizes the two primary metrics with their
confidence intervals. The proposed camera-constrained MAPPO controller reduces mean travel time
by \ImproveFixed\% relative to fixed-time, by \ImproveMaxP\% relative to max-pressure actuation,
and by \ImproveStrongest\% relative to the strongest classical baseline, while also attaining the
lowest P95 waiting time---indicating that the gains are not purchased at the cost of approach
starvation. The improvement over the IPPO ablation isolates the contribution of centralized
training with decentralized execution under shared, non-stationary demand.

\subsection{The Information-Restriction Cost}
\label{sec:restriction_cost}
We isolate the price of the camera-only constraint by comparing the proxy policy against the
otherwise-identical privileged-observation upper bound (the last row of each block in
Table~\ref{tab:main_results}): the reward, architecture, and training budget are held fixed, and
only the state visibility differs, so the residual gap is attributable to the discarded
privileged signals (exact halting counts, per-vehicle waiting times, and network-wide counts).
Restricting the observation to camera-computable features increases mean travel time by only
\RestrictionCost\%, equivalently retaining \RetainPriv\% of the privileged policy's performance.
This cost is small relative to the \ImproveStrongest\% improvement the proxy policy attains over
the strongest classical baseline, supporting the central claim that the camera-observable feature
set retains the overwhelming majority of the task-relevant information for the signal-timing
objective.

\subsection{Robustness to Sensing Noise}
\label{sec:robustness_noise}
To probe whether the camera-computable premise survives realistic perception error, the trained
proxy policy is evaluated under the sensing-noise model of Section~\ref{sec:noise_model}. The base
($1\times$) magnitudes and provenance of every perturbation mode are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:noise_params}; all are fixed on a documented basis except the class-confusion flip
rate, which is measured directly from the YOLOv11 validation-set confusion matrix. Each magnitude
is scaled by a common factor $c\in\{0,0.5,1,2\}$, and Fig.~\ref{fig:noise_degradation} traces the
resulting degradation from clean features ($0\times$) to twice the calibrated envelope ($2\times$).
Performance declines smoothly rather than collapsing, and the relative ordering against the
heuristic baselines is preserved throughout, indicating that the policy does not depend on
high-precision feature recovery.

forms of distributional shift: a motorcycle-share sweep that brackets the Vietnamese field range

medium, high, and asymmetric loading (Table~\ref{tab:generalization}b). Across both, the
camera-constrained policy
sustains its margin over the strongest baseline, indicating that the learned control behavior
does not overfit the calibration operating point.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablations}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} quantifies the marginal value of each design choice by retraining the
policy with one component removed or reverted at a time---never by eval-time reweighting---so that
each arm reflects a fully re-optimized controller. Every component is justified: removing any one
degrades travel time by $10$--$21\%$ and P95 waiting by $20$--$51\%$. The cooperative throughput
term is the single most important signal---removing it inflates P95 waiting by $50.7\%$ and travel
time by $21\%$. Across all arms the tail (P95) inflates roughly twice as fast as the mean, and the
anti-starvation components confirm this pattern: reverting the signed phase-imbalance pressure to
its unsigned form ($+38.4\%$ P95) or the mean-of-squares queue penalty to mean-then-square
($+27.3\%$ P95) inflates tail latency far more than the mean, precisely where a linear queue
average and a one-sided pressure surrogate are blind. The independent-critic IPPO arm ($+3.5\%$
P95) isolates the smaller but consistent benefit of centralized training.
