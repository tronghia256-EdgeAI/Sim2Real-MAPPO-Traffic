\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

We evaluate every controller on the N2 corridor and the N3 grid under the time-varying
peak-demand profile of Section~\ref{sec:networks}, reporting the mean across five training
seeds with $95\%$ confidence intervals and assessing significance against each baseline
with the Mann--Whitney $U$ test under Holm--Bonferroni correction
(Section~\ref{sec:metrics}). The analysis proceeds from the headline comparison
(Section~\ref{sec:res_quantitative}) through the topology dependence of the learned advantage
(Section~\ref{sec:topology}) to the cost of the camera-only restriction
(Section~\ref{sec:restriction_cost}), robustness under sensing noise
(Section~\ref{sec:robustness_noise}), generalization to compositional and demand shift
(Section~\ref{sec:generalization}), and component ablations (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).

\subsection{Main Performance Comparison}
\label{sec:res_quantitative}
Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports the primary and secondary metrics for all methods on the
N3 grid, and Fig.~\ref{fig:main_comparison} visualizes the per-metric comparison with
confidence intervals. On the N3 grid, the proposed camera-constrained MAPPO controller attains
the best result on \emph{every} metric within the camera-deployable class: it reduces mean
travel time by \ImproveFixed\% relative to fixed-time, by \ImproveMaxP\% relative to
max-pressure, and by \ImproveStrongest\% relative to Webster, the strongest camera-deployable
classical baseline, while also attaining the lowest deployable-class P95 waiting time
($419.9$~s against $490$--$548$~s)---indicating that the gains are not purchased at the cost
of approach starvation. Effect sizes against every deployable baseline are large
(Cohen's $|d| \geq 1.13$), although at $n{=}5$ training seeds no pairwise difference survives
the Holm-corrected test ($p_{\mathrm{holm}} = 0.08$--$0.30$); consistent with the protocol of
Section~\ref{sec:metrics}, we report these margins as consistent large-effect trends rather
than certified per-comparison significance. The improvement over the IPPO ablation
($288.5$ versus $299.8$~s mean travel time) isolates the contribution of centralized training
with decentralized execution under shared, non-stationary demand;
Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence} traces the corresponding training curves, where both CTDE variants
reach their reward plateau within ${\sim}50$k steps while the independent-critic policy
requires most of the $300$k-step budget to close the gap.

The instrumented references bound this result from above. The loop-actuated controller
attains a $7.3\%$ lower mean travel time than the camera-only policy ($268.8$ versus
$288.5$~s; $p_{\mathrm{holm}} = 0.17$) and completes $1.1\%$ more trips---the only
Holm-significant difference in the table. Per-second in-road gap sensing therefore remains
marginally competitive with learned camera-only control, and the appropriate reading is that
the proposed stack recovers most, but not all, of the capability of a fully instrumented
intersection while requiring no in-pavement hardware; the privileged MAPPO upper bound
($279.7$~s) sits between the two, and is analyzed in Section~\ref{sec:restriction_cost}. The
corridor evaluation \emph{reverses} this ranking; the reversal is systematic rather than
incidental, and Section~\ref{sec:topology} reports and analyzes it as a result about
topology, not about training.

\subsection{Topology Dependence and the Deployability Trade-off}
\label{sec:topology}
% N2 numbers: results/paper1_mappo/eval_tables/n2_corridor_20260704_105936/summary.json
% (5 training seeds x 5 route seeds; IPPO / privileged were not run on the corridor).
% This prose is the SOLE venue for the corridor numbers (no table row by design).
On the N2 corridor the ranking reverses, and the reversal is itself informative. Webster,
max-pressure, SOTL, and the loop-actuated reference cluster within $5.7\%$ of one another
($94.1$--$99.5$~s mean travel time), whereas the camera-constrained policy attains $118.7$~s:
statistically indistinguishable from fixed-time control ($107.6$~s; $p = 0.69$) but behind
all four classical alternatives by large, Holm-certified margins
($p_{\mathrm{holm}} \approx 0.04$, $|d| \geq 2.0$). We report this outcome without qualification, because the contrast between
the two topologies delineates precisely where learned camera-only control adds value and
where it does not.

The mechanism is structural rather than statistical. A $1\times5$ arterial possesses a single
dominant progression axis, and near-optimal control on such an axis takes the form of a
common-cycle green wave---a solution the classical family produces \emph{by construction}:
Webster through a shared cycle time, gap-actuation by extending green as platoons arrive, and
max-pressure by servicing the through movement that dominates every queue differential. The
corridor consequently operates close to its instrumented ceiling before any learning takes
place: per-second in-road sensing improves on the strongest camera-deployable heuristic by
only $4.1\%$ on N2, against $16.4\%$ on N3. The queue-centric objective family optimized
here---PRESSLIGHT-style pressure terms included~\cite{wei2019_presslight}---is
\emph{instantaneous}: no term rewards inter-junction phase offsets, so a green wave can arise
only as an incidental byproduct, never as an optimization target. Notably, the deficit
persisted even though the corridor arm was granted two standard coordination aids---the
upstream neighbor's phase in the observation and a shared team reward
(Section~\ref{sec:networks})---so it is one of objective structure, not of missing
information: myopic per-junction switching that is locally profitable under a queue objective
can actively fragment platoons that a progression scheme would have preserved. The $4\times4$
grid inverts every one of these conditions. With sixteen interacting junctions and conflicting
crossing flows there is no single axis to progress along, offset-based coordination becomes
ill-posed, and the local queue differential becomes the correct decision variable---there, the
learned policy closes roughly $63\%$ of the headroom separating the strongest
camera-deployable heuristic from the instrumented ceiling (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}).

Two practical conclusions follow. First, the marginal value of learned control grows with
topological complexity. On a low-complexity arterial, classical progression control already
sits near the instrumented optimum, leaving no headroom against which a learned controller
can repay its complexity; at grid-like network cores the headroom is four times larger and
the proposed policy captures most of it. Rather than a one-size-fits-all prescription, this
yields a concrete deployment guideline: retain classical progression control on isolated
arterials, and allocate learned camera-based control to the multi-axis cores where the
classical heuristics degrade. Second, the corridor's best controller remains an instrumented
reference whose advantage is purchased with per-second inductive-loop detection on every
approach---precisely the in-pavement infrastructure the camera-only stack is designed to
eliminate. Under a sensing-cost-matched comparison, the proposed policy is the strongest
member of its sensing class exactly where that class's classical members fail, and it concedes
the corridor to a solution family whose optimality there is structural.

\subsection{The Information-Restriction Cost}
\label{sec:restriction_cost}
We isolate the price of the camera-only constraint by comparing the proxy policy against the
otherwise-identical privileged-observation upper bound (the last row of
Table~\ref{tab:main_results}): the reward, architecture, and training budget are held fixed, and
only the state visibility differs, so the residual gap is attributable to the discarded
privileged signals (exact halting counts, per-vehicle waiting times, and network-wide counts).
On the N3 grid, restricting the observation to camera-computable features increases mean travel
time by only \RestrictionCost\% ($288.5$ versus $279.7$~s), equivalently retaining \RetainPriv\%
of the privileged policy's performance; the two policies' confidence intervals overlap, so the
comparison is reported descriptively. This cost is small relative to the \ImproveStrongest\%
improvement the proxy policy attains over the strongest camera-deployable classical baseline,
supporting the central claim that the camera-observable feature set retains the overwhelming
majority of the task-relevant information for the signal-timing objective.
Fig.~\ref{fig:convergence} shows that this equivalence is not an endpoint artifact: the proxy
policy's training-reward trajectory tracks the privileged variant's throughout the entire
budget, with overlapping confidence bands from the first evaluation onward, indicating that
the information restriction neither slows nor destabilizes learning.

\subsection{Robustness to Sensing Noise}
\label{sec:robustness_noise}
To probe whether the camera-computable premise survives realistic perception error, the trained
proxy policy is evaluated under the sensing-noise model of Section~\ref{sec:noise_model}. The base
($1\times$) magnitudes and provenance of every perturbation mode are listed in
Table~\ref{tab:noise_params}; all are fixed on a documented basis except the class-confusion flip
rate, which is measured directly from the YOLOv11 validation-set confusion matrix. Each magnitude
is scaled by a common factor $c\in\{0,0.5,1,2\}$, and Fig.~\ref{fig:noise_degradation} traces the
resulting degradation from clean features ($0\times$) to twice the calibrated envelope ($2\times$).
Degradation is graceful rather than catastrophic: the worst point of the swept curve
($315.8$~s at $1\times$) stays below the strongest camera-deployable baseline
($321.6$~s, Webster), and doubling every calibrated magnitude still leaves the policy
ahead ($302.7$~s); the residual non-monotonicity of the curve is well within the
seed-level confidence bands. The two structural failures separate cleanly: a one-step
($5$~s) pipeline latency is absorbed at negligible cost ($294.5$~s versus $295.7$~s
clean), whereas silencing an entire approach camera---the harshest single-sensor
failure probed---degrades the policy to parity with, but not below, the best classical
alternative ($321.8$~s). The proxy policy thus fails \emph{soft}: even the worst
perception regime tested recovers classical-baseline performance rather than collapsing
beneath it, so the camera-computable premise does not rest on high-precision feature
recovery.

\subsection{Generalization to Composition and Demand Shift}
\label{sec:generalization}
The policy is trained once at the $73\%$ motorcycle anchor under the trapezoidal demand
profile and evaluated zero-shot under two forms of distributional shift: a motorcycle-share
sweep that brackets the Vietnamese field range (Table~\ref{tab:generalization}a) and an
out-of-distribution (OOD) demand sweep over uniform low, medium, high, and asymmetric loading
(Table~\ref{tab:generalization}b).
% OOD sources: eval_tables/n3_grid_20260705_074219 (low), _081226 (medium),
% _081404 (asymmetric), n3_grid_20260704_235150 (high, ~24k veh). Confirmed via A_ood_*.log.
The demand sweep reproduces, along the load axis, the matched-complexity pattern of
Section~\ref{sec:topology}. Under light uniform demand the classical heuristics suffice and
max-pressure leads ($127.3$ versus $136.2$~s); at medium load the proxy policy draws level
with the best heuristic ($142.0$ versus $143.0$~s); and under saturated demand
($1.67$~veh/s, ${\sim}24{,}000$ vehicles) it becomes the strongest controller in the
comparison outright---ahead of the best camera-deployable baseline ($431.3$ versus
$435.0$~s) and, notably, of the loop-actuated instrumented reference as well ($437.0$~s).
The $7.3\%$ in-distribution advantage of per-second gap sensing
(Table~\ref{tab:main_results}) vanishes and nominally reverses under saturation, when
persistent queues leave the gap-out logic no gaps to act on; differences at $n{=}5$ are not
Holm-significant and are reported descriptively. Under asymmetric loading the policy remains
within $2.6\%$ of max-pressure while clearly ahead of Webster, SOTL, and fixed-time. Across
every regime the policy is never outperformed by fixed-time control, indicating that the
learned behavior does not overfit the calibration operating point---and that its advantage
grows precisely where congestion makes signal timing hardest.

A third axis of shift probes the lateral-dynamics assumption itself. The sublane-trained
policy is evaluated zero-shot on a lane-based variant of the same network and demand:
identical routes and vehicle mix, but with SUMO's default discrete-lane car-following in
place of the $0.4$~m sublane resolution, so that motorcycles lose the filtering behavior
the controller was trained under and effective capacity contracts for every controller
(mean travel times rise to $445$--$518$~s). The proxy policy transfers without retraining:
at $459.7$~s it remains ahead of Webster ($505.3$~s), SOTL ($502.3$~s), fixed-time
($495.9$~s), and---most strikingly---the loop-actuated reference ($517.9$~s), whose
per-second gap-out logic finds no exploitable headways once discrete lane queues saturate,
while trailing max-pressure ($444.8$~s) by $3.3\%$. The camera-computable features
therefore do not encode simulator-specific lateral dynamics, and the mixed-traffic sublane
setting of contribution C4 does not privilege the learned controller.

\subsection{Ablation Study}
\label{sec:ablations}
Table~\ref{tab:ablation} quantifies the marginal value of each design choice by retraining the
policy with one component removed or reverted at a time---never by eval-time reweighting---so that
each arm reflects a fully re-optimized controller. Every reward component is justified: removing
any one degrades travel time by $10$--$21\%$ and P95 waiting by $20$--$51\%$. The cooperative
throughput term is the single most important signal---removing it inflates P95 waiting by $50.7\%$
and travel time by $21\%$. Across the reward arms the tail (P95) inflates roughly twice as fast as
the mean, and the anti-starvation components confirm this pattern: reverting the signed
phase-imbalance pressure to its unsigned form ($+38.4\%$ P95) or the mean-of-squares queue penalty
to mean-then-square ($+27.3\%$ P95) inflates tail latency far more than the mean, precisely where
a linear queue average and a one-sided pressure surrogate are blind.

The observation arms isolate which camera-derived features carry the signal. Removing the two
class-composition shares leaves performance unchanged at the $73\%$ operating point ($289.5$~s,
$\Delta$P95 ${\approx}\,0$): these features are cheap to compute but not load-bearing
in-distribution. Removing the signed pressure \emph{feature} costs little on the mean ($+2.1\%$)
but $+10.8\%$ on the tail, echoing on the observation side the tail-first signature of its
reward-side counterpart. The largest observation-side cost comes from reverting per-approach
aggregation to a raw lane slice that leaves half of each intersection's approaches unobserved
($+5.7\%$ travel, $+9.9\%$ P95)---complete, even if noisy, spatial coverage matters more than any
single engineered feature, which is precisely the regime a low-cost camera ring can provide.
Finally, the independent-critic IPPO arm ($+3.5\%$ P95) isolates the smaller but consistent
benefit of centralized training.

\subsection{Post-Hoc Environmental Indicators}
\label{sec:emissions}
The last two columns of Table~\ref{tab:main_results} report the HBEFA-based
CO\textsubscript{2} and fuel totals (Section~\ref{sec:metrics}); neither quantity is ever
exposed to the agent reward, so these are strictly post-hoc consequences of the learned
timing. The ordering mirrors the
travel-time ranking: the proxy policy yields the lowest emissions in the camera-deployable
class, reducing episode CO\textsubscript{2} by $9.8\%$ against fixed-time and $8.2\%$ against
